# Arcahv — The Delightfulness of Catastrophe

*Feryquitous · Arcaea*

There is something in *Arcahv* that words can hardly capture: a harmonious contradiction of catastrophe and creation, hope and despair, tenderness and cruelty.

It begins with a sacred chant, distant and solemn. Then almost seamlessly, an eerie warmth emerges. It feels as though an angel is singing a lullaby for the world and the people in it, at the very moment she begins to destroy it. And perhaps the most unsettling thought is that she genuinely loves what she is destroying.

The apocalyptic vocal sampling, the fractured yet perfectly organized beats, and the ethereal melodies somehow come together into a singular artistic vision. Everything is falling apart, yet everything seems to be exactly where it belongs. **There is an almost hysterical beauty in this tragedy—an almost unbearable beauty within destruction(in GPT's words), a strange delightfulness in the face of annihilation.**

And then there is Hikari, watching the sky of Arcaea fall apart.

*“Delightful.”*

*Arcahv* is already an extraordinary composition on its own. But placed after that breathtaking scene, with the story of Hikari and Tairitsu behind it, the music becomes something more. The narrative gives the music meaning; the music makes the scene eternal. Whether by miraculous coincidence or ingenious design, two works of art converge into something inseparable, as though they had always been destined to meet.

## A closer listen

Around 0:13, the chant holds B♭4 over E♭1. Across the octaves, the two notes form a perfect fifth. Around 1:23, the voice returns to the same B♭4, this time over E2. The fifth gives way to a tritone. ## From recording to score

The transcription began with the stereo recording. KUIELab MDX-Net separated the voice, bass and drums, leaving a fourth part for instruments and effects. A pitch tracker followed the voice every 40 milliseconds. The bass overtones helped identify its lowest notes, and peaks in the drum track marked rhythmic accents.
